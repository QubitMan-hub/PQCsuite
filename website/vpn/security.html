What Acxelin VPN defends against

It protects traffic between sites, and between people and your network, against an attacker who records it now to decrypt later, one who sits in the middle of a key agreement, and one who holds a certificate you have revoked. Each row below is a behaviour its tests check.

ThreatDefence
Recording IPsec traffic to break it laterIKE's key exchange is hybrid ML-KEM, and a post-quantum PPK is mixed in. Real tunnels between network namespaces are tested in CI.
Recording WireGuard traffic to break it laterEach computer's pre-shared key comes from a post-quantum key agreement and is replaced every two minutes.
A man in the middle of the key agreementTLS 1.3 with hybrid ML-KEM and ML-DSA certificates from your authority on both sides. A classical or wrong-role peer is refused.
Replaying a key agreementKeys come from that session's exporter, bound to both names and a fresh tag.
A revoked site or personNo new keys, and the tunnel is cut within 15 seconds of the revocation list reaching the gateway. An expired list refuses everyone.
Traffic leaking while a full tunnel is downThe kill switch blocks it. An upgrade replaces the previous release's kill switch with no gap.
Secrets in an exportThe CBOM records PSK and PPK type and size only. A test searches it for every key, token and passphrase as bytes, hex, base64 and PEM.

The console and the desktop app

Local only
Both listen on 127.0.0.1 and refuse requests whose host name is not a loopback name or whose origin is another site, so a web page you visit cannot reach them.
Launch links
Each opens from a link that works once, kept in the address fragment so it never reaches a server or a log. The session uses no cookies, and after ten wrong attempts in a minute an address is refused.
Passphrases
The authority's passphrase is held in the console's memory and never written. The app never offers to save a computer's passphrase, and only its background service runs with administrator rights.
Audit
Invitations and revocations are written to an owner-only log, which never holds an invitation's token.

What is not covered

  • WireGuard's own handshake is classical; the post-quantum part is the pre-shared key mixed into it.
  • Whoever holds the authority's key can issue certificates and join the VPN. Keep it encrypted, and off the gateways.
  • Whoever uses an invitation first enrols with it. Send it through a channel you trust.
  • Malware on a laptop or gateway sees traffic before it is encrypted.
  • Phones are not supported, and on Windows the kill switch can lift for a moment while the tunnel is recreated.
  • Tunnels were exercised in Linux network namespaces with strongSwan 6.1 and OpenSSL 3.5.9, and on GitHub's Windows and macOS runners, not on customer networks.

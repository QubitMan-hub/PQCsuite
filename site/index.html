Why enterprises must act now

The threat is already here. Quantum is just the deadline.

A quantum computer that breaks RSA and elliptic curves does not exist yet. The data it will read is being recorded now, and the replacement algorithms are already standardised.

Harvest now, decrypt later

Encrypted traffic captured today can be decrypted after Q-Day. Anything that must stay secret for years is already exposed.

Regulatory deadlines are set

NIST IR 8547 deprecates quantum-vulnerable public-key algorithms and later disallows them. NSA CNSA 2.0 sets dates for each type of system.

RSA and ECC at 112-bit security: deprecated 2030, disallowed 2035

Long-lived keys outlive the plan

Root certificates, VPN gateways and backup archives stay in service for a decade. What you issue and encrypt now has to survive the transition.

You cannot migrate what you cannot see

Most teams cannot say which endpoints still negotiate classical key exchange. Migration starts with measuring it.

Questions

What teams ask us first

Do we have to change our applications?

No. The TLS edge sits in front of the service and forwards plain traffic to it, as a gateway or a Kubernetes sidecar. Clients that cannot speak post-quantum TLS yet reach the service through a second edge next to them.

What about web browsers?

Current browsers already negotiate X25519MLKEM768, but they cannot verify ML-DSA certificates yet. Under the transition policy the edge sends them a classical ECDSA or RSA certificate, and post-quantum clients get ML-DSA on the same port.

Why hybrid rather than post-quantum only?

ML-KEM and ML-DSA are new. Pairing each with a proven classical algorithm means an attacker has to break both, so a weakness found in either one does not expose your data.

How fast does revocation take effect?

A revoked certificate is refused at its next TLS handshake. VPN tunnels and remote users whose certificate is revoked are cut within 15 seconds, and a peer that stops proving itself loses its tunnel after three key-rotation periods.

Can the CA key live in an HSM or a cloud KMS?

Yes. Issuing CAs can sign with ML-DSA keys in AWS KMS or through any HSM that has a command-line signing tool. Roots can be hash-based SLH-DSA and kept offline.

Is it FIPS 140-3 validated?

No. It implements the FIPS 203, 204 and 205 algorithms through OpenSSL 3.5 and pyca/cryptography, but the suite itself has not been through FIPS 140-3 validation.
